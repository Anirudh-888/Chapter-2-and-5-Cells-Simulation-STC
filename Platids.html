Fluid-filled matrix inside inner membrane. Contains Calvin Cycle enzymes, circular DNA, ribosomes, starch granules.

Grana (Thylakoid Stacks)

Stacked disc-like thylakoid membranes. Each stack = a granum. Light reactions occur here.

💧

Thylakoid Lumen

Inner fluid space of thylakoids. H⁺ ions accumulate here during light reactions to drive ATP synthesis.

🌿

Chlorophyll

Green pigment in thylakoid membranes. Absorbs red (~680nm) and blue (~430nm) light to power photosynthesis.

🔗

Stromal Lamellae

Unstacked connecting membranes linking grana. Allow material flow between granum stacks.

🧬

Chloroplast DNA

Circular DNA in the stroma. Evidence that chloroplasts evolved from ancient cyanobacteria (endosymbiosis).

Step-by-Step: PS II to PS I
1

Photon Absorption (PS II)

Chlorophyll in Photosystem II absorbs a photon (~680nm). This excites an electron to a higher energy level.

2

Water Splitting (Photolysis)

To replace the lost electron, water is split: 2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂. Oxygen is released as a byproduct!

3

Electron Transport Chain

Excited electrons travel through Plastoquinone, Cytochrome b6f, Plastocyanin, pumping H⁺ ions into the thylakoid lumen.

4

Photosystem I Re-energizes

Electrons at PS I are re-excited by another photon (~700nm), then reduce NADP⁺ to form NADPH.

5

ATP Synthesis (Chemiosmosis)

H⁺ ions flow back through ATP Synthase from the lumen to stroma (like a turbine), generating ATP from ADP + Pi.

🌈 Why Plants are Green?

Chlorophyll absorbs Red (~680nm) and Blue (~430nm) light. It reflects green light, making leaves appear green.

💧 Photolysis of Water

Every 2 water molecules split releases 1 O₂ molecule. All oxygen in Earth's atmosphere came from photolysis!

⚡ Chemiosmosis

Proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane drives ATP Synthase — the same mechanism as in mitochondria.

3 Stages of the Calvin Cycle
1

Carbon Fixation

CO₂ combines with RuBP (5-carbon) via enzyme RuBisCO to form two molecules of 3-PGA (3-phosphoglycerate).

2

Reduction

3-PGA is reduced using ATP and NADPH to form G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate), the direct sugar building block.

3

Regeneration of RuBP

Most G3P molecules are used (with ATP) to regenerate RuBP so the cycle continues. 1 in 6 G3P exits to form glucose.

Types of Plastids

Plastids are organelles found only in plant cells and algae. Three main types based on pigment and function.

🌿

Chloroplasts

Contain chlorophyll (green pigment). Found in leaves. Carry out photosynthesis. Most important plastid.

🌸

Chromoplasts

Contain carotenoids (red, orange, yellow). Found in flowers, fruits, roots. Attract pollinators.

⬜

Leucoplasts

Colourless plastids. Store food: amyloplasts store starch, elaioplasts store oils, aleuroplasts store proteins.

🔄

Interconversion

Plastids can convert between types. Tomatoes ripening: chloroplasts → chromoplasts (green → red). Potato in light: leucoplasts → chloroplasts.

🧬

Endosymbiotic Origin

Chloroplasts evolved from ancient cyanobacteria engulfed ~1.5 billion years ago. Evidence: double membrane, own DNA, own ribosomes.

📏

Size and Number

Chloroplasts are 4–10 μm long. A single cell may have 10–100 chloroplasts. They move to maximize light capture.

Chlorophyll Absorption Spectrum

Chlorophyll a and b absorb specific wavelengths of light. Green light is reflected, making plants appear green.
